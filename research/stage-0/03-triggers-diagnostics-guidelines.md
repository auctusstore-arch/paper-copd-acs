## Trigger framework

### Muller 1989

Muller JE, Tofler GH, Stone PH. Circadian variation and triggers of onset of acute cardiovascular disease. *Circulation.* 1989;79(4):733-743.

- DOI: https://doi.org/10.1161/01.CIR.79.4.733
- PMID: https://pubmed.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov/2647318/
- Type: historical review.

The paper reviews morning concentration of acute cardiovascular events and plausible haemodynamic, autonomic, platelet, and fibrinolytic mechanisms. It supports the general transient-trigger framework. It is not a primary estimate of COPD-related risk.

### Mittleman and Mostofsky 2011

Mittleman MA, Mostofsky E. Physical, psychological and chemical triggers of acute cardiovascular events: preventive strategies. *Circulation.* 2011;124(3):346-354.

- DOI: https://doi.org/10.1161/CIRCULATIONAHA.110.968776
- PMID: https://pubmed.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov/21768552/
- Type: narrative review of acute cardiovascular triggers.

The review describes short hazard periods after transient exposures in susceptible hosts. Absolute burden depends on exposure prevalence and baseline risk. It provides the general conceptual precedent for the manuscript's trigger component.

## Respiratory infection as a myocardial infarction trigger

### Smeeth et al. 2004

Smeeth L, Thomas SL, Hall AJ, Hubbard R, Farrington P, Vallance P. Risk of myocardial infarction and stroke after acute infection or vaccination. *N Engl J Med.* 2004;351(25):2611-2618.

- DOI: https://doi.org/10.1056/NEJMoa041747
- PMID: https://pubmed.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov/15602021/
- Design: self-controlled case series.
- Population: 20,486 patients with first MI and 19,063 with first stroke.
- Respiratory infection, days 1 to 3: MI incidence ratio 4.95, 95% CI 4.43-5.53; stroke incidence ratio 3.19, 95% CI 2.81-3.62.
- Location: PubMed abstract Results.

### Warren-Gash et al. 2012

Warren-Gash C, Hayward AC, Hemingway H, et al. Influenza infection and risk of acute myocardial infarction in England and Wales: a CALIBER self-controlled case series study. *J Infect Dis.* 2012;206(11):1652-1659.

- DOI: https://doi.org/10.1093/infdis/jis597
- PMID: https://pubmed.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov/23048170/
- PMCID: https://pmc.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov/articles/PMC3488196/
- Design: self-controlled case series using linked health records.
- Highest estimate during days 1 to 3 after an acute respiratory infection consultation: incidence ratio 4.19, 95% CI 3.18-5.53.

The exposure was largely record-based. Do not label every case laboratory-confirmed influenza.

### Kwong et al. 2018

Kwong JC, Schwartz KL, Campitelli MA, et al. Acute myocardial infarction after laboratory-confirmed influenza infection. *N Engl J Med.* 2018;378(4):345-353.

- DOI: https://doi.org/10.1056/NEJMoa1702090
- PMID: https://pubmed.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov/29365305/
- Design: self-controlled case series with laboratory-confirmed influenza.
- MI during days 1 to 7: incidence ratio 6.05, 95% CI 3.86-9.50.
- Influenza B: 10.11, 95% CI 4.37-23.38.
- Influenza A: 5.17, 95% CI 3.02-8.84.
- Location: PubMed abstract Results.

These studies establish a general trigger literature that predates the COPD two-clock model. The manuscript should reposition novelty around testing substrate and trigger processes within the same COPD population, not around inventing the trigger concept.

## Pulmonary embolism in AECOPD

Aleva FE, Voets LWLM, Simons SO, de Mast Q, van der Ven AJAM, Heijdra YF. Prevalence and localization of pulmonary embolism in unexplained acute exacerbations of COPD: a systematic review and meta-analysis. *Chest.* 2017;151(3):544-554.

- DOI: https://doi.org/10.1016/j.chest.2016.07.034
- PMID: https://pubmed.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov/27522956/
- Seven studies, 880 patients with unexplained AECOPD.
- Pooled PE prevalence: 16.1%, 95% CI 8.3-25.8%.
- Pooled DVT prevalence: about 10.5%.

The estimate applies to unexplained exacerbations and has substantial heterogeneity. It must not be presented as prevalence across all AECOPD.

## Type 2 myocardial infarction and coronary disease

### JAMA 2017 troponin study

Chapman AR, Lee KK, McAllister DA, et al. Association of high-sensitivity cardiac troponin I concentration with cardiac outcomes in patients with suspected acute coronary syndrome. *JAMA.* 2017;318(19):1913-1924.

- DOI: https://doi.org/10.1001/jama.2017.17488
- PMID: https://pubmed.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov/29127948/
- PMCID: https://pmc.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov/articles/PMC5710293/

This source concerns high-sensitivity troponin and prognosis in suspected ACS. It does not provide systematic coronary imaging in type 2 MI.

### DEMAND-MI

Bularga A, Hung J, Daghem M, et al. Coronary artery and cardiac disease in patients with type 2 myocardial infarction: a prospective cohort study. *Circulation.* 2022;145(16):1188-1200.

- DOI: https://doi.org/10.1161/CIRCULATIONAHA.121.058542
- PMID: https://pubmed.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov/35341327/
- PMCID: https://pmc.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov/articles/PMC9010024/
- Design: prospective cohort with systematic coronary and cardiac imaging.
- CAD was present in about 68%; obstructive CAD in about 30%.

The manuscript's `roughly half` statement requires correction and a citation to DEMAND-MI, not the JAMA 2017 paper. The selected cohort does not support routine immediate angiography for every patient with type 2 MI.

## Troponin during AECOPD

### Brekke et al. 2009

Brekke PH, Omland T, Holmedal SH, Smith P, Soyseth V. Determinants of cardiac troponin T elevation in COPD exacerbation: a cross-sectional study. *BMC Pulm Med.* 2009;9:35.

- DOI: https://doi.org/10.1186/1471-2466-9-35
- PMID: https://pubmed.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov/19615100/
- PMCID: https://pmc.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov/articles/PMC2718858/
- Population: 441 AECOPD admissions.
- cTnT at least 0.04 micrograms/L: 120 patients, about 27%.

### Hoiseth et al. 2011

Hoiseth AD, Neukamm A, Karlsson BD, Omland T, Brekke PH, Soyseth V. Elevated high-sensitivity cardiac troponin T is associated with increased mortality after acute exacerbation of chronic obstructive pulmonary disease. *Thorax.* 2011;66(9):775-781.

- DOI: https://doi.org/10.1136/thx.2010.153122
- PMID: https://pubmed.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov/21653926/
- Design: prospective observational cohort.
- hs-cTnT above the 99th percentile occurred in about three quarters of the cohort and showed a graded association with mortality.

### McAllister et al. 2012

McAllister DA, Maclay JD, Mills NL, et al. Diagnosis of myocardial infarction following hospitalisation for exacerbation of COPD. *Eur Respir J.* 2012;39(5):1097-1103.

- DOI: https://doi.org/10.1183/09031936.00124811
- PMID: https://pubmed.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov/22323574/
- Population: 242 patients hospitalised with AECOPD.
- About 10% had elevated troponin and 8.3% met the Universal Definition of MI from elevated troponin plus chest pain and/or serial ECG changes.

These peer-reviewed sources should replace the Cureus review when the manuscript discusses frequency, prognosis, or adjudicated MI. Troponin elevation is common and prognostic, but a single positive value does not identify type 1 MI.

## Diagnostic standards

### Fourth Universal Definition of MI

Thygesen K, Alpert JS, Jaffe AS, et al. Fourth universal definition of myocardial infarction (2018). *Eur Heart J.* 2019;40(3):237-269.

- DOI: https://doi.org/10.1093/eurheartj/ehy462
- PMID: https://pubmed.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov/30165617/

Myocardial injury requires troponin above the 99th percentile. Acute injury requires a rise or fall. MI requires acute injury plus evidence of ischaemia. This consensus should anchor the distinction among injury, type 1 MI, and type 2 MI.

### ESC ACS 2023

Byrne RA, Rossello X, Coughlan JJ, et al. 2023 ESC Guidelines for the management of acute coronary syndromes. *Eur Heart J.* 2023;44(38):3720-3826.

- DOI: https://doi.org/10.1093/eurheartj/ehad191
- PMID: https://pubmed.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov/37622654/
- Society page: https://www.escardio.org/Guidelines/Clinical-Practice-Guidelines/Acute-Coronary-Syndromes-ACS-Guidelines

The guideline supports prompt 12-lead ECG, serial ECG when suspicion persists, and validated serial high-sensitivity troponin algorithms. A nondiagnostic initial ECG does not exclude NSTE-ACS. Routine oxygen is not recommended when oxygen saturation is at least 90%; oxygen is indicated for hypoxaemia.

### AHA/ACC Chest Pain 2021

Gulati M, Levy PD, Mukherjee D, et al. 2021 AHA/ACC/ASE/CHEST/SAEM/SCCT/SCMR Guideline for the Evaluation and Diagnosis of Chest Pain: A Report of the American College of Cardiology/American Heart Association Joint Committee on Clinical Practice Guidelines. *Circulation.* 2021;144(22):e368-e454.

- DOI: https://doi.org/10.1161/CIR.0000000000001029
- PMID: https://pubmed.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov/34709879/

This guideline supports rapid ECG, serial ECG when needed, and serial high-sensitivity troponin. It is a stronger reference than the current low-quality ECG prevalence paper.

### ACC/AHA ACS 2025

Rao SV, O'Donoghue ML, Ruel M, et al. 2025 ACC/AHA/ACEP/NAEMSP/SCAI Guideline for the Management of Patients With Acute Coronary Syndromes: A Report of the American College of Cardiology/American Heart Association Joint Committee on Clinical Practice Guidelines. *Circulation.* 2025;151(13):e771-e862.

- DOI: https://doi.org/10.1161/CIR.0000000000001309
- PMID: https://pubmed.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov/40014670/
- Official hub: https://www.acc.org/Guidelines/Hubs/Acute-Coronary-Syndromes

The oxygen recommendation is consistent with ESC guidance: treat hypoxaemia and avoid routine oxygen in patients without it. The final PDF should be used to supply the exact table and page in the revised manuscript.
