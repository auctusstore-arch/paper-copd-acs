## Summary Recommendation

**Primary target: CJC Open (Canadian Journal of Cardiology Open), Review article.**  
**Secondary target: European Heart Journal Open (EHJ Open), Review article.**  
**Tertiary target: Frontiers in Cardiovascular Medicine, Review article.**  
**Respiratory fallback: ERJ Open Research or Chest (Contemporary Review).**

Rationale: The author is a cardiologist (Department of Cardiology and Vascular Medicine, Universitas Airlangga). Cardiology journals should be prioritised over respiratory journals. The manuscript's cardiology content (beta-blocker reversal, type 2 MI, troponin, anti-inflammatory trials, influenza vaccination, oxygen therapy) is substantial and central, not peripheral. The two-clock model is a cardiovascular risk framework applied to COPD, not a respiratory framework.

Among cardiology journals, the highest-impact titles (EHJ main, Circulation, JAMA Cardiology, JACC main) commission reviews by invitation; unsolicited single-author narrative reviews almost never pass desk screening. The realistic cardiology targets are open-access or hybrid journals that accept unsolicited narrative reviews and have published COPD-cardiovascular content: CJC Open, EHJ Open, Frontiers in Cardiovascular Medicine, Heart (BMJ), and American Heart Journal.

CJC Open is the primary target because the style exemplar (Heffernan & Rutherford, CJC Open 2025, ref [65]) is a narrative review of COPD and cardiovascular disease at the same register and length as this manuscript. The journal has confirmed scope fit and acceptance precedent. EHJ Open is the secondary target: it is the ESC's open-access journal, accepts unsolicited reviews, and has published cardiopulmonary reviews. Frontiers in Cardiovascular Medicine is the tertiary target: it has published 29 COPD reviews including COPD-AF and COPD-pulmonary hypertension reviews, confirming strong scope fit for cardiopulmonary overlap content.

- **JACC (main journal)** — IF ~24. Commissions State-of-the-Art Reviews and Reviews by invitation. Not viable.
- **European Journal of Heart Failure** — IF ~8. Has 87 COPD papers and 9 COPD reviews, but scope is heart-failure-specific. The manuscript's coronary focus (ACS, type 2 MI, beta-blockers) is secondary to the heart failure scope. Not a natural fit unless the manuscript is reframed around COPD-heart failure overlap.

## Submission Strategy

### Cardiology-first pathway (recommended)

1. **First choice: CJC Open.** Submit as a Review article. No word cut needed. The exemplar paper (Heffernan & Rutherford 2025, ref [65]) confirms acceptance of this exact manuscript type, length, and register. The author is a cardiologist; CJC Open is a cardiology journal. APC is USD 2,715, the lowest among realistic open-access cardiology targets. CCS may consider waivers for low/middle-income country authors (Indonesia qualifies).

2. **If CJC Open declines: EHJ Open.** Submit as a Review article. No word cut needed. ESC affiliation provides editorial standards and prestige. Higher impact factor than CJC Open (~6.0 vs ~3.0). APC is EUR 2,273. The manuscript's European guideline citations (ESC chronic coronary syndromes) strengthen the fit.

3. **If both decline: Frontiers in Cardiovascular Medicine.** Submit as a Review article. No word cut needed. Direct scope match: ref [4] (Marriott et al. on AMI-COPD inflammation) was published here. APC is CHF 3,150. Frontiers may be more receptive to single-author submissions than the ESC or CCS journals.

4. **If all three decline: Heart (BMJ) or American Heart Journal.** Heart requires ~30-40% condensation; AHJ requires minor trimming. Heart is hybrid (subscription or OA at ~GBP 3,000+); AHJ is subscription (no APC). Both have moderate impact factors (~5.1).

### Respiratory fallback pathway (if cardiology journals are not receptive)

5. **ERJ or Chest.** If all cardiology targets decline, submit to ERJ as a Review article or Chest as a Contemporary Review. Both have high impact factors (ERJ ~9.3, Chest ~9.5) and strong scope fit. ERJ may require reclassification or condensation; Chest is a near-fit for word count. These are the highest-impact options but are respiratory journals, not cardiology journals.

6. **ERJ Open Research.** No word cut needed. Same ERS editorial standards. Open access APC is the main barrier; ERS may waive for low/middle-income country authors.

### Optional split

7. **Diagnostic corollary split (optional):** Section 8 (diagnostic corollary) could be extracted as a standalone Viewpoint for a cardiology journal (JACC: Advances or CJC Open), but this would weaken the parent manuscript and is not recommended unless the main paper is rejected.
