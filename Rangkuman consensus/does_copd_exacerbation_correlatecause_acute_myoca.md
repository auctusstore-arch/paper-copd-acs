Multiple large self-controlled case series, meta-analyses, and multinational cohort studies consistently
demonstrate a marked, temporally clustered increase in acute coronary events following COPD exacerbations. The
evidence supports both correlation and a likely causal contribution, though distinguishing true plaque-rupture
events from oxygen supply-demand mismatch (type 2 MI) remains a challenge.

Temporal Risk and Severity Gradient
The landmark self-controlled case series by Donaldson et al. (2010), analyzing 25,857 COPD patients, established a
2.27-fold increased MI risk in the 1–5 days immediately following an exacerbation, with risk diminishing
progressively thereafter (Donaldson et al., 2010). This temporal clustering was replicated and extended by Rothnie
et al. (2018), who found MI risk elevated for 91 days post-exacerbation, with severe exacerbations producing an IRR
of 2.58 versus 1.58 for moderate events (Rothnie et al., 2018). The SUMMIT trial post hoc analysis confirmed a 3.8fold hazard for composite cardiovascular events within 30 days, rising to 9.9-fold after hospitalized exacerbations
(Kunisaki et al., 2018).
A 2023 multinational cohort study across four countries found acute coronary syndrome hazard ratios ranging from
5.59 to 24.57 in the first 7 days post-exacerbation, remaining elevated for 30 days (Hawkins et al., 2023). Graul et
al. (2023) reported a composite cardiovascular aHR of 3.19 in the first 14 days, with severe exacerbations driving an
aHR of 14.5 (Graul et al., 2023). The 2025 meta-analysis by Pirera et al. confirmed significantly increased ACS risk
peaking at 1–30 days and persisting beyond one year (Pirera et al., 2025).

• Among patients hospitalized for severe AECOPD, 18.75% experienced an acute cardiovascular event during

The consistency of the temporal signal across self-controlled designs—which eliminate time-invariant confounding
by smoking, age, and comorbidities—argues for a genuine causal contribution of exacerbations to acute coronary
events, though the relative contribution of type 1 versus type 2 MI mechanisms requires further delineation.
Preventing exacerbations may decrease the risk of subsequent acute cardiovascular events (Müllerová et al., 2022;
Graul et al., 2023).
